Deep dive: where dimensional modelling fits

Where dimensional modelling fitsAdded

Silver = enterprise integration model. Standardized canonical entities, identifiers, relationships, source mappings and conformed attributes. It may be normalized or otherwise structured for integration and reuse.

Gold = analytical consumption model. Publish business-process-oriented fact and dimension tables, commonly as star schemas/data marts, with agreed grain and reusable conformed dimensions.

Do not treat “conformed dimension” and “dimensional model” as synonyms: a conformed dimension is a shared business definition; the Gold star schema is the analytical structure that uses it. SCD processing can occur wherever the dimension is maintained.

Logical architecture with pattern callouts

L1 Sources
ERP/GL, CRM, OMS/EMS, trading, billing, market feeds, Excel. Capture owner, entities, volume, refresh, integration options.
L2 Ingestion
Batch · CDC · API · streaming · events · files · document OCR. Choose by capability, volume, latency. No business logic here.
L3 Raw (Bronze)
Immutable source copy, schema history, ingestion timestamp, provenance. Enables replay and audit.
L4 Refinement (Silver)
Validate, cleanse, deduplicate, standardize, reconcile. Mappings: source→enterprise CoA, cost center, legal entity, customer ID, currency, period. Effective-dated and versioned.
L5 Trusted data
Canonical models, conformed definitions (maintained in Silver/MDM, consumed by Gold stars), data contracts. SCD Type 1 and 2 for change history.
L6 MDM and reference
Match, golden record, attribute-level survivorship, hierarchies, stewardship, cross-reference. Start with legal entity, CoA, cost center, currency, period.

L7 Data products (Gold)
Financial, customer, trading, subscription, operational. Owner, contract, versioning, quality SLA, access policy, lineage.

Deep dive: Gold facts, dimensions and grain

Gold analytical modelling: facts, dimensions and grainAdded

  • Start with the business process and grain. State exactly what one fact row represents before choosing keys or measures. Example: one row per posted GL journal line.
  • Fact tables hold measurable events or states and foreign keys to dimensions. Types: transaction, periodic snapshot (e.g. month-end account balance), accumulating snapshot (e.g. close workflow), and factless fact (e.g. eligibility/coverage).
  • Dimension tables describe who/what/where/when/how: Date, Company, Legal Entity, Account, Cost Center, Currency, Customer, Product. Use surrogate keys; retain source/natural keys as business identifiers.
  • Star schema is the default analytical pattern: dimensions filter/group facts. Snowflake dimensions normalize subdimensions where justified, but add joins and complexity.
  • Conformed dimensions share the same business meaning, keys/mappings and hierarchies across subject areas and facts. Use a Kimball bus matrix to plan which dimensions are shared by which business processes.
  • Other patterns: role-playing dimensions (Order Date / Posting Date), degenerate dimensions (document number), junk dimensions (small flags), bridge tables (many-to-many), and hierarchy/parent-child dimensions.
  • Measures: additive (debit/credit amounts), semi-additive (account balances across time), non-additive (ratios/percentages). Calculate ratios from additive components rather than summing them.
Deep dive: financial model example

Financial model exampleAdded

Fact_GL_Transaction — grain: one posted GL journal line. Dimension keys: Date, Company, Legal Entity, Account, Cost Center, Currency (plus relevant Customer/Product). Measures: debit, credit, signed local amount, reporting-currency amount. Journal/document number can be a degenerate dimension.

Separate facts at separate grains: Fact_Monthly_Account_Balance (one account/entity/currency/period), Fact_Budget (budget line/period), Fact_Forecast, Fact_Intercompany_Transaction and an FX rate reference table (date/currency-pair grain). Never join facts of different grains directly and assume the result is valid; aggregate each to a conformed dimensional grain first.

Finance dimensions: Dim_Date, Dim_Company, Dim_Legal_Entity, Dim_Account (local CoA mapped to enterprise CoA), Dim_Cost_Center, Dim_Currency, Dim_Customer and Dim_Product. Preserve local account codes and effective-dated mappings.

Deep dive: SCD and late-arriving data

Dimension history and late-arriving dataAdded

  • SCD 0: retain original; Type 1: overwrite correction; Type 2: new surrogate-key row with effective-from, effective-to and current flag; Type 3: retain limited prior value. Prioritize Types 1 and 2.
  • For Type 2, facts resolve the dimension surrogate key valid at the fact event date. Define treatment for late-arriving dimensions, inferred/unknown members, early-arriving facts, retroactive corrections and backfills.
  • Keep auditability: source system/key, effective dates, load timestamps, change reason and mapping version where required.

Deep dive: Power BI modelling and query mode

Power BI modelling and query modeAdded

  • Prefer a well-designed star schema with one-to-many dimension-to-fact relationships and single-direction filtering by default. Use bidirectional or many-to-many relationships only with a clear requirement and tested filter behavior.
  • Use role-playing dates with active/inactive relationships where appropriate; use explicit DAX measures for governed calculations and calculated columns only when row-level attributes are needed.
  • Import generally gives strong interactive performance; DirectQuery queries the source and depends on source concurrency/latency; composite mixes storage modes; Direct Lake can be considered in a suitable Fabric architecture. Choose from freshness, scale, latency, concurrency, governance and cost requirements—not user count alone.
  • Use incremental refresh and aggregation tables for large facts where appropriate. Reuse certified semantic models; apply RLS/OLS and test security paths.
  • Keep business definitions in governed measures/semantic models. CFO and finance owners must approve the EBITDA calculation, adjustment policy and reporting currency rules.

Choosing the engineAdded

Databricks

Heavy engineering, Spark, ML, multi-cloud, open formats.

Fabric

Microsoft-centric estate, Power BI first, SaaS operations, quicker start.

Snowflake

SQL-centric teams, low platform operations, strong sharing.

Decide on: existing estate, skills, workload mix, cost model, time-to-value, lock-in. For the first 90 days use a thin slice (one workload subscription, minimal guardrails), then harden the landing zone in year 1.

Running 6-7 engagements in parallelAdded

  • Same six-stage playbook and reusable accelerators (connectors, mapping templates, canonical models, DQ rules, ADRs, dashboards).
  • Each engagement has a lead architect and a delivery lead. I own the sponsor relationship and quality gates.
  • Weekly portfolio review: value KPIs, risks, staffing, escalations.
  • Step in on decisions (scope, conflict, risk), not daily design.

Engagement shape and team (adapt)Added

  • Phase 1: fixed 90-day outcome. Later phases gated on value KPIs, with change control.
  • Typical pod: engagement lead, principal architect, 2-3 data engineers, finance SME/analyst, BI developer, PM.
  • Reuse lowers effort per engagement and protects margin.
  • Effort and price ranges: confirm against firm norms.

Regulated data and securityAdded

  • Data residency, classification, masking, retention, access logging and audit evidence by design.
  • Policy guardrails at landing zone level; data-level controls in Unity Catalog.
  • Confirm exact regulatory requirements with the client’s compliance team. Do not assume them.

AI-ready foundationAdded

  • Certified Gold data, governed semantic definitions and lineage are the prerequisites.
  • Start with low-risk use cases: variance commentary, forecasting, anomaly detection, with human review.
  • Control what AI can see: same access policies as BI.

Challenge questionsAdded

Core: architecture and trust

How do you prove EBITDA is trusted?Reconcile to the audited GL, show lineage from reported number to source, govern the definition, and have finance sign off each period.
Why not buy a consolidation tool?Often right for statutory consolidation. The foundation still matters because management reporting, KPIs and later analytics need the same trusted data. I would integrate, not duplicate.
What if the companies refuse a common chart of accounts?Keep local CoAs, map them to an enterprise CoA with effective-dated mappings, and converge over time.
How do you stop this becoming a multi-year program?Ship the 90-day wedge, measure value KPIs, and fund the next domain only on proven outcomes.
Central or federated?Central guardrails, platform and standards; federated domain ownership of data products and quality.
An acquisition lands mid-project?Reuse source onboarding, mappings and the conformed model so onboarding is configuration, not redesign.
Why this platform?Context decides: existing estate, skills, workload mix, cost, time-to-value. The patterns hold on any platform.

Core: leadership and commercial Added

How do you run 6-7 engagements in parallel?One playbook and reusable accelerators, a lead architect and delivery lead per engagement, a weekly portfolio review of value KPIs, risks and staffing. I own sponsor relationships and quality gates, and step in on decisions.
The CFO wants results in 30 days and the CTO wants the platform first. What do you do?Offer a thin slice: one workload, trusted EBITDA first, platform hardening in parallel. Agree decision rights up front and show both sponsors the same value KPIs.
How do you handle a controller who defends their Excel process?Learn what the spreadsheet solves, keep the logic as governed adjustments with approval and audit trail, show time saved, and get the CFO to sponsor the change.
How do you protect scope and margin?Fixed 90-day outcome, change control, phase funding gated on value KPIs, and accelerators to cut effort. Pricing and staffing follow firm norms.
What does “AI-ready” mean here?Certified Gold data, governed semantics, lineage and the same access policies as BI. Start with variance commentary and forecasting, with human review.
How do you handle regulated data?Residency, classification, masking, retention and audit evidence by design. Confirm specific regulator requirements with the client’s compliance team.
Deep dive: modelling questions
How do you design the financial dimensional model?Start with the process and grain: one GL journal line per fact row. Add conformed Date, Company, Legal Entity, Account, Cost Center and Currency dimensions. Keep balances, budgets and forecasts in separate facts at their own grains.
Why not put the star schema in Silver?Silver is the integrated, standardized enterprise representation; Gold is shaped for analytical consumption. The boundary is a design choice, but keep canonical integration concerns separate from business-process facts and dimensions.
How do you handle chart-of-accounts differences?Preserve local CoA identifiers, map to an enterprise account dimension using effective-dated mappings, retain lineage and steward mapping exceptions.
How do you implement SCD Type 2?Detect tracked attribute changes, expire the current row, insert a new surrogate-key version with effective dates/current flag, and resolve each fact to the version valid on its event date. Define late-arriving and retroactive correction handling.
How do you prevent double counting across facts?Declare and document each fact's grain. Aggregate facts independently to a common conformed dimensional grain before combining measures; never join transaction facts to periodic snapshots naively.
How do you choose Import vs DirectQuery in Power BI?Assess freshness, query latency, concurrency, source capacity, model size, governance and cost. Prefer Import when refresh latency is acceptable; use DirectQuery/composite/Direct Lake only when requirements and platform capabilities justify them.

30-minute discovery: SOLVEAdded

15 questions, 5 blocks, 3 each. Business first, technology last. Ask each company separately, then compare. If time runs short, drop V and keep S and O.

S 5O 7L 8V 6E 4

S: Strategy (why?)

  • Decision: What one decision or report must the CFO and sponsor trust within 90 days?
  • Thesis: What is the investment thesis and hold period?
  • Add-ons: Are more acquisitions planned, and how fast must they onboard?

Decides: the 90-day wedge and how reusable the design must be.

O: Official numbers (what must be trusted?)

  • Close: How long does the monthly close take, and where does it hurt?
  • EBITDA: How is it defined in each company, and where do manual adjustments live?
  • Chart: Do the companies share a chart of accounts, entities, currencies and calendar?

Decides: mapping and consolidation scope, and the need for a governed adjustment layer.

L: Landscape (where is the data?)

  • Systems: Which systems feed finance, who owns them, and do customer and entity IDs match?
  • Access: Can we extract by API, database, files or CDC?
  • Sensitivity: What data is regulated or has residency rules?

Decides: ingestion patterns, MDM scope and security constraints.

V: Vehicle (how will we build it?)

  • Stack: What cloud, data platform and BI tools exist today?
  • Skills: What skills does the team have, and is there a buy-vs-build preference?
  • Autonomy: Do the companies stay independent, or does everything centralize?

Decides: platform choice and central vs federated governance.

E: Execution (who and when?)

  • Owner: Who signs off the EBITDA definition and owns each source?
  • Blockers: What could stop us in the first 30 days (access, approvals, people, licences)?
  • Measure: How will we measure success (close days, adjustments removed, onboarding time)?

Decides: decision rights, risks and value KPIs.
